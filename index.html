01 — School management system

Dasmariñas North NHS
Website & Portal

Full-stack — frontend, database design and authentication

Live site

The problem

The school needed two things from one system. Outside the login: a public site that worked on a phone, carrying enrolment dates, campus information and class shifts. Inside it: a way to move a grade from a teacher's class record, through the adviser who has to confirm it, and out to the student — with someone able to see, at any point, which of the school's hundred classes had actually handed anything in.

What I built

  • Three role-based portals — student, teacher and administrator, each with its own navigation, permissions and dashboard, behind one PHP session login.
  • A grade approval workflow — teachers upload marks per term, advisers confirm them, and students only see a grade once it's published. Every class carries its state: not submitted, awaiting review, returned, or published.
  • DepEd-accurate grading — three terms per subject with computed finals against the 75 passing line, honours tracked toward 90, and the Observed Values rubric (Maka-Diyos, Makatao, Makakalikasan, Makabansa) recorded per term.
  • An analytics dashboard — school-wide average and passing rate, grade distribution across five bands, per-subject and per-section rankings with failing counts, and submission progress over a rolling 14 days.
  • Multi-year records and admin tooling — data filed by school year with archives, activity logs, and a one-click .sql export so the database can be backed up and restored through phpMyAdmin.
  • Accessibility in every portal — a three-step text-size control and a dark mode, available to students, teachers and admins alike rather than just on the public site.

What was hard

The first version let a teacher edit a grade after it had already been approved and published, and the change went straight through to the student. The approval step only ran on first submission, so the entire review process could be skipped just by editing afterwards — I only caught it testing with a teacher and a student account open side by side. The fix was to stop treating approval as a one-time flag and make the grade's status a real state instead: editing a published grade drops it back to awaiting adviser review and hides it from the student until it's confirmed again. What I took from it is that a workflow isn't finished when the happy path works. It's finished when you know what every edit does to the state.

02 — Student tool

GWA Calculator &
Honours Tracker

Solo — vanilla JavaScript, no libraries

Try it live

The problem

Every student works their general average out on a phone calculator, and the question they actually want answered isn't "what is my average" — it's "what do I need next quarter to make honours?" Nothing answers the second one.

What I built

  • Live general average — enter grades per quarter, get rounded subject finals and a running general average that updates as you type.
  • Honours standing — checked against all the criteria, including the rule that no single subject may fall below 85.
  • A reverse planner — pick a target and it tells you the lowest grade you'd need in every box you've left blank.
  • Saves as you go — everything persists in localStorage, so nothing is lost on refresh and no data ever leaves the device.

What was hard

The planner looks like simple algebra until the rounding gets in the way. Subject finals are rounded to whole numbers before they're averaged, so the general average is a step function of your next grade, not a straight line — solving it algebraically gives answers that are off by one at exactly the boundaries people care about. With three quarters at 90 and a target of 90, the algebra says you need 90; you actually only need 88, because 89.5 rounds up. So I stopped solving it and started searching it: try every whole grade from 0 to 100, recompute the whole result each time, return the first that works. 101 iterations is nothing, and it's exact by construction rather than approximately right. I verified it against 400 randomly generated grade boards, asserting each answer was genuinely the minimum.

03 — Group project

Catering Booking
Website

Team of students — my part: front-end and database design

The problem

A catering business taking bookings by phone and message has no reliable record of what was ordered for which event, and customers can't see what's on offer without asking for it first.

What the team built

  • A public catalogue of catering menus and packages with pricing.
  • Date and time selection for the customer's occasion.
  • A booking request form capturing the event details.
  • An admin side for managing menus and packages, viewing bookings, and seeing booking and sales summaries.

Bookings were requests rather than purchases — there was no online payment, and the caterer arranged that directly.

What I did

  • Front-end — the menu and package pages, the booking form, and the responsive layout.
  • Database design — the MySQL tables for menus, packages and bookings, and the relationships between them.
